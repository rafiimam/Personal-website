SUMMARY: This report outlines the development and validation of the DocXAR-CUFIT framework, designed to mitigate structured OCR noise in
Multimodal Document Understanding (MDU). By integrating Layer-Wise Sensitivity Masking, Cross-Attention Adapters, and a Decoupled Quality
Estimator, the framework recovers significant performance degradation caused by noisy inputs. Multi-seed evaluations on the FUNSD dataset under
50% synthetic OCR corruption demonstrate a highly stable Mean Test F1 Score of 0.7992 ┬▒ 0.0067, offering a paradigm-shifting improvement over
standard noisy LoRA adaptation (~0.6334).
1. PROBLEM FRAMING AND RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS
The evolution of Multimodal Foundation Models (MFMs) has necessitated a strategic pivot from addressing classical "noisy-label" learningΓÇöwhere
ground-truth labels are corruptedΓÇöto the more arduous domain of noisy-input adaptation. Within Multimodal Document Understanding (MDU),
structured OCR noise represents a critical failure point. Unlike stochastic pixel noise, OCR degradation selectively erodes the linguistic coherence of
tokens while preserving visual-spatial layout features, creating a modality misalignment that standard Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)
methods fail to adjudicate.
Standard LoRA updates weights uniformly without regard for the reliability of input modalities, leading to a "garbage-in, garbage-out" propagation
across the transformer backbone. DocXAR-CUFIT addresses this by combining cross-attention adapters with layer-wise sensitivity masking,
enforcing a uniform 25% active parameter density to isolate clean visual-spatial signals and prevent rank collapse under severe text degradation.
2. TECHNICAL METHODOLOGY: REFINEMENTS IN DOCXAR-CUFIT
Maintaining model stability during the fine-tuning of 12-layer transformer blocks requires precise calibration of gradient flow. Without structural
constraints, models often succumb to global gradient starvation, where specific layers saturate early. DocXAR-CUFIT introduces three core
architectural mechanisms:
(cid:127) Layer-Wise Sensitivity Masking: Enforces a uniform 25.0% active parameter density across all transformer blocks via tensor-by-tensor
thresholding.
(cid:127) Cross-Attention Adapter: Connects question embeddings directly to document key-value pairs after the final encoder block, filtering out
non-relevant corrupted spans.
(cid:127) Decoupled Quality Estimator Q(x): Evaluates per-sample input corruption under torch.no_grad() with batch normalization to prevent weight
collapse.
